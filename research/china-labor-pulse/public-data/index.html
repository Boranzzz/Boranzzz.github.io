Reading Labor-Market Pressure Through Buffer Sectors: Evidence from Guangzhou Ride-Hailing

Prototype · 14 Nov 2026

Building a monthly index of labor-market pressure in Guangzhou from ride-hailing, the buffer people turn to when other work pays less or becomes harder to find. The aim is to see how much of that turn is a weaker outside option, and how much is driving itself becoming a better deal.

Model

Participation depends on the gap between the return to driving and the outside option. More labour supplied at a given driving return requires a lower outside option.

rₜ = bₜ + a + λ ln(Nₜ / Mₜ), λ > 0 θ₀ = q₀ / λ Iₜ = 100 × { ln[(Nₜ/N₀) / (Mₜ/M₀)] / θ₀ − (zₜ/z₀ − 1) − uₜ }

r is the net return for a standardized active day. b is the outside employment return in the same units, wage plus the chance of finding the job. N is daily-average active drivers, a proxy for labour input. M is the pool of potential workers. z is revenue per vehicle times active vehicles, divided by active drivers. q is receipts after platform fees and relevant subsidies, before operating costs. θ₀ is the elasticity of labour input with respect to q at the January 2024 reference. It is not estimated from this participation series. u is any extra net-return change, as a share of baseline q₀: lower costs or a better take-home than z shows. Positive u reduces inferred pressure.

Of an increase in participation, the part explained by a better driving return stays in the receipts term. The rest is the decline in b required to rationalize it. The index rises when inferred b falls relative to January 2024, and it stays high if the outside return stays low. It does not return to zero merely because deterioration stops. January 2024 is the reference month, not a month with zero distress. Iₜ is 100 times outside-return loss over q₀. It is not an unemployment rate.

No smoothing, seasonal adjustment, or coverage correction. Positive means a lower inferred outside return than at the reference date.

Public data

Monthly reports publish active drivers and gross receipts, not hours or deductions, and they do not define “active.” March–June 2026: drivers 106,100 to 108,300 (+2.07%), gross-receipts proxy −CNY 54.90 per driver-day (−13.58%). That is a table pattern, not yet hours or net return. 单车日均营收 is not net of commission.

Net return

A commission ceiling, an 18.8% multi-province median, rental templates, and a few court contracts show that deductions exist. None is a Guangzhou monthly series for this window.

Labour input

Hour evidence is annual bins or other cities. It does not give hours per Guangzhou active driver-day in March and June 2026.

Result

Inconclusive on external pressure until the same city and platform universe publishes hours and the definition of active, realised payout after commission and bonuses, and major costs by rental type.
