Measuring Local Labor-Market Pressure from a Buffer Sector: Guangzhou Working Prototype

Research note · Working version · 11 Nov 2026

Are outside employment opportunities deteriorating, and by how much? This prototype reads participation and receipts in ride-hailing together, using a simple inverse-supply relationship.

Scenario assumptions

Industry labor-input elasticity with respect to receipts q after effective platform fees and relevant subsidies, before operating costs. Neither the default of 1 nor the slider range is independently calibrated.
Percent of baseline q₀. Positive values mean net returns improved beyond what the receipts proxy captures, for example through lower costs. A linear path from zero at the reference date; not observed data.
M changes linearly from the reference date. This adjusts participation N/M; it does not correct platform reporting. Default: unchanged.

Model

Participation depends on the gap between the return to driving and the outside option.

rₜ = bₜ + a + λ ln(Nₜ/Mₜ), λ > 0 θ₀ = q₀/λ Iₜ = 100 × { ln[(Nₜ/N₀)/(Mₜ/M₀)]/θ₀ − (zₜ/z₀ − 1) − uₜ }

r is the net return for a standardized active day; b is the outside employment return in the same units. More labor supplied at a given return requires a lower outside option under the maintained supply relationship. Better driving returns can attract labor without outside deterioration.

N is daily-average active drivers, a proxy for labor input. z is revenue per vehicle y × active vehicles V ÷ active drivers N. Its denominators must be compatible. q is receipts after effective platform fees and relevant subsidies, before operating costs.

The index uses a fixed January 2024 reference. If inputs and returns remain unchanged, pressure stays at its existing level. It does not return to zero simply because deterioration stops.

The log inverse-supply function is imposed explicitly. These are exact finite differences within that model, not accumulated local approximations. θ₀ is an elasticity at the reference point only.

u is additional net-return change / q₀. Setting u=0 maintains proportional q and z and unchanged operating-cost levels. Positive u reduces inferred pressure. Comparable hours, stable selection and preferences, and the mapping from realized receipts to expected returns remain assumptions. λ, costs and the mapping are not independently established, so no yuan-denominated loss is reported.

Coverage

Seven reports state that all operating platforms uploaded data; 22 list non-uploading platforms; one does not disclose upload status. The consecutive runs are July 2024, April 2025, and February–June 2026.

An upload statement does not establish completeness. No upload does not prove that active business was omitted. Platform size and cross-platform driver overlap are unknown; platform-count ratios cannot correct activity.

February 2026 is affected by Spring Festival, as the report explicitly notes. We retain the full run, add March–June, and retain April 2025–April 2026, the sole same-month annual pair with both endpoints stating all platforms uploaded. No seasonal adjustment is implied.

Seasonality has not been ruled out. Receipts fell in the same window in both prior years. The 2026 decline is larger. Historical changes also contain macro trends, shocks and reporting changes; they are not pure seasonal components.

At θ₀=1, the two-year average leaves 12.39 points under level changes or 11.65 under proportional changes. With 2024 alone and θ₀=0.2, the sign reverses. The result is not robust to every positive elasticity. Earlier endpoints list non-uploading platforms; comparability remains unresolved. Only two prior windows are available, with no significance or forecast test.

Same-month annual comparisons remove fixed month effects under the relevant specification, but not evolving seasonal patterns or all moving-holiday effects. No X-13/STL or month-dummy seasonal fit is used. This diagnostic does not replace the fixed-reference level index: subtracting historical pressure could remove genuine persistent deterioration.

Use

Use the prototype to inspect how signs depend on assumptions, calculate the adjustment needed to reverse a conclusion, and prioritize independent evidence. The displayed scenarios do not establish how much Guangzhou's outside opportunities actually deteriorated.

No hiring, job-loss or other external employment series was used to choose these parameters. External validation and holdout prediction remain unfinished. Historical-window comparisons are diagnostics, not a replacement for the fixed-reference level index.
